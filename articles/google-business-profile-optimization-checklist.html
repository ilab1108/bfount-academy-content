To optimize a Google Business Profile, get verified, pick the most accurate primary category, fill in every field that applies to you (services, hours, attributes, description, photos), and then keep the profile active with fresh reviews, review replies, posts and photos every month. Google says local results are ranked mainly on relevance, distance and prominence. You cannot change distance, but almost everything on this checklist improves relevance (how well your profile matches the search) or prominence (how well known and trusted your business looks).

Below is our 32-point checklist, with the time each step takes. It is written for real local businesses and for marketers who manage profiles for clients.

1. How does Google rank local results?

Google's own help page on local ranking names three main factors:

  • Relevance — how well a profile matches what someone is searching for. Complete, accurate information (categories, services, description) helps Google understand what you do.
  • Distance — how far the business is from the searcher, or from the location named in the search.
  • Prominence — how well known the business is. Google says this draws on information from across the web, including links, articles and directories, and that review count and ratings play a part.

Google also states that there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking, so be wary of anyone selling "guaranteed Map Pack positions". You win by being the most complete, accurate and active profile near the searcher.

2. Before you start: the rules that get profiles suspended

A suspended profile disappears from Maps, and reinstatement can take weeks. Most suspensions come from a handful of mistakes, so check these first:

  • Use your real business name. Do not add keywords or locations to the name ("Smith Plumbing – Emergency Plumber Austin") unless that is your legal or signage name. Keyword-stuffed names are a common reason for suspension and a common target for competitor edits.
  • Only one profile per real location. Virtual offices, co-working desks you never staff and PO boxes are not eligible addresses.
  • Service-area businesses hide their address. If you visit customers (cleaners, electricians, mobile mechanics) and do not serve them at your address, set a service area and do not show the street address.
  • Never buy or incentivize reviews. Offering discounts for reviews, or asking only happy customers ("review gating"), breaks Google's policies. Google has also tightened review spam detection in 2026 and can pause new reviews on a profile when it sees suspicious spikes.
  • Lead generators: a profile for a business that does not physically exist at that address is a fake listing. If you generate calls for others, use websites and paid search instead (see our guide to local SEO and Google Business Profile for inbound calls).

4. Categories, services and description: getting relevance right

Choose the primary category carefully

The primary category is the strongest relevance signal you control. Choose the most specific category that describes what most of your revenue comes from. A dental clinic that mostly does implants might still choose "Dentist" if that is what most patients search for, then add "Dental implants periodontist" or similar only if it is accurate. To see what works in your area, search your main service in Google Maps and look at the categories shown under the top results.

Additional categories and services

Add extra categories only for services you really offer. Every extra category should match a real page on your website and real jobs you take. Then fill in the Services section: list each service with a one or two sentence description using the words customers use ("boiler repair", not "heating solutions").

Writing the description

You get up to 750 characters, and the first 250 or so show before "more". Use this simple template:

[Business name] is a [category] serving [areas] since [year]. We help [who] with [top 3 services]. [One sentence of proof: licences, guarantees, years, number of jobs you can verify]. [How to get started: call, book online, visit].

Do not put links, prices that change often, or promotional phrases like "best in town" in the description. Keep it factual.

5. Photos and videos

Photos help people decide, and they help Google confirm what and where your business is. Google's guidance encourages businesses to add photos and videos to show what they offer. Practical rules:

  • Use real photos taken on a phone in good light. Stock photos add little and can look untrustworthy.
  • Show the entrance and signage so first-time visitors recognize you.
  • For service-area businesses, show vans, uniforms, the team and finished work.
  • Add new photos regularly rather than 50 on day one; a steady stream keeps the profile looking current.
  • Check customer-uploaded photos occasionally and report anything inappropriate.

6. Reviews: how to get more and how to reply

Review count, rating and recency are part of prominence, and they are also what a searcher reads before calling. The simplest system is to ask every customer, at the moment they are happiest, with a direct link.

Review request template (text message)

Hi [Name], thanks for choosing [Business] today. If you have a minute, an honest review helps other people in [town] find us: [short link]. Thank you! — [Your name]

Ask everyone, not only customers you expect to be happy, and never offer anything in return.

Reply templates

Positive review: "Thanks, [Name]. Glad the [service] in [area] went smoothly — [one specific detail from their review]. We're here if you need us again."

Negative review: "Hi [Name], I'm sorry this wasn't the experience you expected. I'd like to put it right — please call me directly on [number] or reply here. — [Owner name], [Business]." Keep it short, calm and specific, and never share private customer details in public.

Replies show future customers how you treat people. Google's help page notes that positive reviews and helpful replies can help a business stand out.

7. Posts, products and keeping the profile active

Posts come in three types — updates, offers and events — each with an optional photo and button. They do not replace a website, but they show the profile is managed and give searchers a reason to act now. Easy post ideas:

  • A seasonal reminder ("Book your AC service before the summer rush").
  • A recent job with a photo and a one-line result.
  • A limited offer with a clear end date.
  • An answer to a question customers ask on the phone every week.

Write posts like short ads: one benefit, one proof point, one action. Our Copywriting That Sells course covers the formulas.

8. What changed recently (Q&A, chat and Ask Maps)

If you follow older guides, three things are out of date:

  • Chat and call history: Google ended the built-in Business Profile chat and call history in July 2024. Put your phone number and booking link front and center instead.
  • Questions & Answers: Google phased out the public Q&A section in late 2025. Industry reports describe it being replaced by "Ask Maps", a Gemini-powered feature that answers questions about places using information such as your profile, reviews and website. That makes a complete profile and a clear website FAQ more important, because they are what the answers draw from.
  • Insights became Performance: the report is available to verified profiles and opens from Google Search or the Maps app.

Google changes features often, so treat any guide (including this one) as a starting point and check the profile editor itself for the options available in your country.

9. Measuring results: calls, directions and website clicks

The Performance report shows interactions such as calls, direction requests, website clicks and bookings, plus the searches that surfaced your profile. Two extra steps make the data far more useful:

  1. UTM-tag the website link, for example ?utm_source=google&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=gbp, so profile visits appear as their own source in GA4 instead of blending into organic search.
  2. Track phone calls. If you use a call tracking number, set it as the primary number and keep your main number as an additional number, so your listing stays consistent. Frequently asked questions

How long does it take for Google Business Profile optimization to work?

Fixes to name, category and hours are usually reflected within days once Google accepts the edits. Gains from reviews, photos and website pages build over weeks and months. There is no fixed timeline, and results depend heavily on how strong nearby competitors are.

Should I put keywords in my business name?

No, unless the keywords are part of your real business name. Adding keywords breaks Google's guidelines and can lead to suspension. Put your services in categories, the services list and the description instead.

Can I rank in a city where I don't have an address?

Distance is a ranking factor, so a profile ranks most easily near its location. A service-area business can list the areas it serves, but listing an area does not guarantee visibility there. For towns further away, build a strong service page on your website and consider local search ads.

How many reviews do I need?

There is no magic number. Look at the top three businesses for your main search in Maps and aim to match their count and beat their recency. A steady flow of genuine reviews every month is more convincing than a burst.

Do Google Business Profile posts help rankings?

Google does not list posts as a ranking factor, but they keep the profile looking active and can turn more viewers into callers. Treat them as conversion tools, not a ranking trick.

What should I do if my profile is suspended?

Fix whatever broke the guidelines (name, address, duplicate listings), gather proof such as a business licence, utility bill and photos of signage, then file an appeal from the profile. Do not create a new profile while the appeal is open.
