A media buyer spends a company's advertising budget on platforms like Google, Meta and TikTok and is judged on one thing: whether that spend comes back as profitable leads, calls or sales. You can become one without a degree or prior job in marketing. The fastest route is to learn one ad platform properly, run small real campaigns with your own money or a friend's business, document the numbers, and use that proof to land an agency, in-house or performance-marketing role.

This guide walks through what the job actually involves, the skills that matter, a 90-day plan to go from zero to your first paid work, and how to prove you can do it when you have no experience on your CV.

1. What does a media buyer do?

Day to day, a media buyer turns a budget and a goal into campaigns, then keeps adjusting them until the numbers work. A typical week includes:

  • Planning: choosing platforms, audiences and budgets based on the offer and the target cost per lead or sale.
  • Launching: building campaigns, ad sets and ads; setting up tracking so every conversion is recorded.
  • Testing: running new creatives, headlines, audiences and landing pages against each other.
  • Optimising: pausing what loses money, moving budget to what works, and adjusting bids.
  • Reporting: explaining results in plain numbers — spend, conversions, cost per result and return on ad spend (ROAS).

The job sits between creative and analytics. You don't need to design the ads yourself, but you need to know which ad to make next and why.

3. The skills that actually matter

Hiring managers rarely ask about certificates first. They ask what you've spent, what you got back, and what you changed when something didn't work. The skills behind those answers are:

  1. One platform in depth. Pick Meta Ads or Google Ads first. Know account structure, bidding, audiences and the main reasons campaigns fail.
  2. Tracking. Pixels, conversion events, UTM parameters and GA4. If you can't measure a result, you can't optimise it.
  3. Unit economics. Knowing your break-even cost per lead or ROAS before you spend. Our max CPL calculator does this in seconds.
  4. Creative judgement. Spotting which hook, image or video angle to test next, and writing clear briefs for designers or AI tools.
  5. Testing discipline. Changing one thing at a time, giving tests enough budget and time, and not reacting to one bad day.
  6. Communication. Explaining results to a client or manager in two sentences, including what you'll do next.

5. How to get experience when you have none

  • Run ads for a local business. Offer a restaurant, gym or clinic a free two-week test where they pay the ad spend and you manage it. Agree the goal and how you'll measure it before you start.
  • Promote something of your own. A small digital product, a newsletter or a lead magnet gives you a full funnel you control end to end.
  • Join an affiliate or pay-per-call network as a publisher. You'll learn real unit economics fast, because the payout is fixed and your ad cost isn't. Start with tiny budgets and strict stop-loss rules.
  • Take on an assistant role. Many agencies hire junior "ads assistants" to build campaigns and pull reports. It's the most common entry point into the job.
  • Practise without spending. Use practice labs to write ads and calculate targets, like the Google ad grader and ROAS calculator.

6. Building a portfolio that gets interviews

One honest, detailed case study beats ten vague claims. Structure each one like this:

  1. The goal: "Get booked consultations for a physiotherapy clinic below a target cost per booking."
  2. The setup: platform, audience, budget, offer and tracking.
  3. What happened first: the starting numbers, including what didn't work.
  4. What you changed: the two or three decisions that moved results, with the reasoning.
  5. The result: spend, conversions, cost per result and what you'd do next.

Add screenshots of the ad account (with any client names hidden) and a short video walkthrough. Put the case study on your LinkedIn profile and link it in every application. A verified course certificate on LinkedIn helps too, especially for your first role.

7. Where to find your first role or client

  • Agencies directly: message the head of paid media at small agencies with your case study and one idea for one of their clients.
  • Freelance platforms: start with narrow, well-defined jobs (campaign setup, tracking fixes, audits) to collect reviews.

8. Common beginner mistakes

  • Learning every platform at once. Get genuinely good at one, then add a second.
  • Spending before tracking works. Test every conversion event before you launch.
  • Not knowing the target. Decide your maximum cost per lead or minimum ROAS before the first dollar is spent.
  • Killing tests too early. One or two days of data is rarely enough to judge an ad.
  • Scaling too fast. Raise budgets in steps and watch cost per result as you go.
  • Ignoring compliance. Ad policies and consent rules (especially for calls and leads) can shut down an account. Learn the basics in our affiliate compliance course.

9. FAQ

Do I need a degree to become a media buyer?
No. Employers care far more about whether you can show real campaign results and explain your decisions.

Which platform should I learn first?
Meta Ads if you're interested in e-commerce, consumer offers or lead generation with visual creative. Google Ads if you prefer search intent, local businesses and calls.

How much money do I need to practise?
Enough for a small daily budget over two to three weeks. Treat it as the cost of training, set a hard limit, and track every result.

Is media buying a good career?
It suits people who like numbers, testing and fast feedback. Strong media buyers can move into senior paid-media roles, run their own agency, or buy traffic for their own offers.

Can AI replace media buyers?
Ad platforms already automate bidding and targeting. That makes the human parts — choosing the offer, the creative angle, the tracking and the budget rules — more valuable, not less. Learn to use AI tools as part of the job; our AI ad creatives course shows how.
